Step 01 of 06Getting acquainted
Stage 01 · Getting acquainted

First consultation

We begin by discussing your concerns, expectations and priorities. An examination helps outline the situation and identify questions that need closer attention.

With your doctor

We discuss symptoms, previous treatment, tooth condition and bite features. For children, we consider development and readiness to participate.

Your part

Bring existing scans and reports, if available. Write down your questions. You do not need new investigations in advance.

An understanding of your situation and an agreed next step.

Your story matters more than any template.

Stage 02 · Understanding

Assessment

We gather the information needed for a clinically justified plan. The extent of assessment depends on clinical indications and is not the same for everyone.

With your doctor

We analyse clinical photographs and digital models or impressions. X-rays and other investigations are prescribed when appropriate and necessary.

Your part

Discuss your health and previous treatment during your in-person consultation. Do not send medical records through the website’s open form.

A diagnostic foundation for a personal treatment plan.

Sound decisions begin with attention to detail.

Stage 03 · Your plan

Individual treatment plan

We bring clinical possibilities together with your needs. Before making a shared decision, we discuss aims, options, limitations, risks, estimated timing and cost.

With your doctor

We explain which changes are appropriate and whether preparation or collaboration with other specialists is needed. The plan may be refined during treatment.

Your part

Ask about alternatives, care and visit frequency. You have time to understand the options and make a considered decision.

An agreed plan and clear expectations, without promises of a guaranteed outcome.

When each step is clear, moving forward feels calmer.

Stage 04 · Your method

Choosing an orthodontic appliance

Braces, aligners or other orthodontic appliances are chosen after assessment. Clinical indications and your ability to follow recommendations matter alongside aesthetic preferences.

With your doctor

We explain the appliance, adaptation, hygiene and instructions for use. Teeth and gums are prepared first when needed.

Your part

Consider your daily routine. Consistent wear is especially important for removable appliances; care and monitoring visits matter for every method.

A clinically justified appliance choice and instructions for starting treatment.

Treatment should suit you — your bite and your daily life.

Stage 05 · Moving forward

Treatment and monitoring

Regular visits help assess progress and make timely adjustments. Your feedback, hygiene and adherence to recommendations are important parts of the process.

With your doctor

We monitor tooth movement, oral health and appliance function. Visit frequency is individual; the plan is refined if the clinical situation changes.

Your part

Follow the wear schedule and care instructions. If your appliance is damaged or you experience unusual pain, contact the doctor before your planned visit.

Consistent support and timely treatment adjustments when needed.

Significant changes grow from consistent small steps.

Stage 06 · Maintaining progress

Completion and retention

After the active phase, it is important to help teeth maintain their new positions. Retention is an integral part of the orthodontic plan, rather than an optional addition.

With your doctor

We select retainers, explain how to use them and agree on follow-up visits. We discuss your individual risk of teeth shifting again.

Your part

Wear retainers as recommended and attend check-ups. Tell your doctor if a fixed retainer loosens or a removable retainer no longer fits.

Individual advice for long-term follow-up and retention.

Care for your smile continues after active treatment.

This is a general introduction. The sequence, investigations, methods and duration are determined individually. No stage guarantees a specific outcome.

Care between visits

our shared attention.

Daily hygiene

Caring for your teeth, gums and appliance remains important at every stage. Discuss suitable hygiene products with your doctor.

Consistency

Follow the recommendations for wearing appliances and attending check-ups. Small daily habits support planned treatment.

An open conversation

Ask questions and share how you feel. If you experience unusual pain or damage your appliance, contact your doctor before the next scheduled visit.
